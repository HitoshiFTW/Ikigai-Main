## 2. Pack 285.7 -- SBC Substrate Flip: KILLED (Premise Falsified)

The locked plan's first priority was to flip the cat-4 substrate from FHRR to Sparse Block Codes (SBC), folding facts into the body to shrink the external cache -- the small-device story. Before wiring anything, two probes tested the load-bearing assumptions.

**Probe 1 -- superposition.** Bundling N slot-add-bound SBC HVs into one vector and recovering by per-block argmax returned **0% at N = 10..500**, while an FHRR bundle held its classic ~20-item wall (96% at N=50, 60% at N=100). SBC has no analogue of FHRR's conjugate-rotate unbind: superposing facts collides every block (L=10 slots, ~N/10 facts per slot), so argmax recovers noise. The premise "fold many facts into one body HV" is structurally impossible for SBC.

**Probe 2 -- noise.** SBC per-fact cleanup is fast and exact at zero noise but collapses under additive phasor noise, and the memo's planned rescue -- per-block redundancy and voting -- does **not** fix it: a 9-fold redundant vote at sigma=0.3 scored 2%, and 0% at sigma>=0.5, against FHRR's 100% at all sigma. Sparse codes are fundamentally noise-fragile; FHRR's density is precisely why it is robust. This is a textbook trade-off, not a tunable parameter.

Production queries are noisy by construction (approximate state match, drift). Flipping to SBC would regress recall. SBC's only genuine win -- constant-time cleanup over a large vocabulary at zero noise (~145 us at any V vs FHRR's 9.6 ms at 10k) -- is already covered by the Pack 273 cache and the 285.5 codebook. Prince's call: close 285.7, keep FHRR as the noise-tolerant substrate, and pursue the small-device goal through compositional derive-not-store instead. The negative result is logged so the flip is never retried.

## 3. Pack 304 -- Compositional Substrate v0 (Derive-Not-Store)

The principle, stated by Prince at Day 78 close: store only irreducible ATOMS (arbitrary facts that cannot be computed, e.g. capital(France)=Paris); DERIVE relations and compositions at query time instead of caching them. This is the math ring (derive any sum, store none) generalised from arithmetic to relations.

The win is combinatorial. From n atomic country attributes the engine answers O(n^2) composite facts -- same-continent comparisons, attribute inheritance, multi-hop chains -- with zero cache entries for the derived answers. A new module `ikigai/cognition/compositional.py` `CompositionEngine` provides `atom(rel, entity)` (read-only cache lookup via canonical question templates) and `derive(text)` (structural relation-algebra rules: inheritance `continent o capital == continent`, same-attribute comparison, numeric comparison, attribute chains). Honesty is preserved by keeping the rules as grammar (relation algebra), not facts -- no country or continent is named in the module; every concrete value comes from a cache atom.

It was wired native in `general_reasoner.reason()` as `do_derive=True` (default), dispatched after arithmetic and before multi-hop, so composites are DERIVED rather than served from storage. The migration was the real demonstration: 21 `continent(capital)` composites (Pack 293 bootstrap, two phrasings = 42 cache entries) were dropped and replaced by 21 fundamental `continent(country)` atoms; the cache went 1613 -> 1592 while answerable composites went up. Gate: continent-of-capital 10/10, same-continent 210/210, multi-hop 10/10 all via `method=derive`, ZERO cache growth across 226 derivations, 30/30 intact, 10.8x derive:store ratio. A `__delitem__`/`pop` was added to CompactAnchorCache for the drop. Saved; backup recorded.

## 5. Pack 305 -- Rule Discovery v0

Where 304 applied authored composition rules, 305 made the organism MINE its own. Built on the existing `schema_inducer.py` (Plotkin least-general-generalisation anti-unification), a new `rule_discovery.py` `RuleMiner` searches a hypothesis space of rule schemas -- inheritance `r_out(link(x)) == r_tgt(x)`, synonymy `r_a(x) == r_b(x)` -- over the organism's atom store, promoting rules by support and confidence. This is inductive logic programming: we supply the rule SHAPES, the organism discovers which concrete rules hold. Inventing new shapes is out of scope and stated as the honest boundary.

`CompositionEngine` gained an enumerable AtomStore (`triples`/`entities`/`relations`, self-populating on every `atom()` hit -- no curated lists), a `learned_rules` store persisted via a `_comp_state` entry added to integrate.py's `_PERSIST_ATTRS`, an `atom_del` for self-compression, and a `require_learned` gate. The killer demonstration is self-compression: the miner discovered `continent o capital` (support=12, conf=0.75) from co-present atoms; with `require_learned=True` the discovered rule drove derivation 24/24, while clearing the rules dropped it to 0/24 -- proving the discovery is load-bearing, not the authored regex; then the 12 now-redundant `continent(capital)` atoms were dropped (1640 -> 1628) with recall intact. The organism learned a regularity and shrank its own memory. Rule and atom index persist across reload (verified). Saved.

## 6. Pack 305.1 -- Rule Discovery v1 (Substrate-Native HV Rules + Inverse Schema)

Three additions. **Substrate-native HV rules:** each learned rule lives in the body as a bound phasor HV (`role(type) (x) role(field0) (x) role(field1)`, blake2b-seeded unit phasors, FHRR Hadamard bind); `rebuild_rule_bank`/`rule_match` do cosine cleanup; `sanctioned_inheritance` routes through the HV bank. Round-trip verified at sim=1.000; HV-sanctioned derivation 12/12; emptying the HV bank dropped it to 0/12 -- the HV rules are load-bearing. **Native autonomous discovery:** `IkigaiOrganism.discover_rules()` mines from the organism's own atom index with link-versus-attribute auto-classified from store structure (`classify_relations`: a relation is a LINK if its values are themselves subjects) -- no external lists; hookable into the sleep/idle tick. **Richer schema:** `mine_inverse` discovered the round-trip rule `inverse capital o country` at conf=1.00 (a genuinely new shape), and the miner correctly REJECTED bad candidates at conf=0.00.

Prince enforced "no hardcoding" a second time here: the gate's ground truth was de-hardcoded -- math truth computed in Python, capital regression by a before/after stability snapshot rather than a literal dict. A real persistence bug was also fixed: `_load_state` had been reloading triple keys as character-tuples (`tuple("a\tb")`), corrupting the atom index on every reload. End: learned_rules=3, HV bank=3, all persist. Saved.

## 7. Pack 306 -- Fast Path + CRT Factored Decode (Facts < 1 ms)

Prince's demand: "facts can be done in less than 50 ms." Profiling showed `reason()` was running the expensive cat-4 `recall_action` (142s cold rebuild / 77ms hot + a 9.6k-vocabulary codebook matmul) on every call, even when a cheap path would answer. Two fixes. A **fast path** (`_fast_answer`) resolves arithmetic, derivation, multi-hop, and exact-cache hits before the heavy machinery, returning early; the heavy ICL/active-learn path runs only when nothing cheap and confident resolves. And in `residue_hdc.py`, a **CRT factored decode** (`add_factored`/`sub_factored`/`decode_factored` via per-modulus `_residue_of` + CRT, O(K*d)) replaced the O(range*d) brute cosine decode that cost ~300 ms per arithmetic call. Result: cache hit 0.06 ms, arithmetic 0.69 ms, derivation 0.03 ms, full 30/30 in 10 ms total (0.3 ms/query) -- the 50 ms target beaten by ~150x. Math remained exact.

## 8. Signed-RHC -- Math Ring Signed-Complete

Pack 304.2 (below) surfaced a real limitation: RHC subtraction returned positive CRT residues, so a-b < 0 decoded wrong. The fix is substrate-native: the modular ring natively represents -k as prod-k, so `decode_factored(signed=True)` maps the upper half (x > prod/2) to its negative. `math_eval` decodes subtraction signed; add/mul/div stay unsigned. Gate: 3-12 = -9, 5-20 = -15, 7-8 = -1, positives unchanged, ring intact. The arithmetic ring is now add + sub(signed) + mul + div, all substrate-native, all complete.

## 13. Pack 287.5 v3 (Negative) + v4 -- Data Quality

The visible junk in 301 prompted a verify-before-absorb attempt. v3 first tried hardcoded badword/length filters (Prince flagged it), then pivoted to a doctrine-clean teacher self-confirmation (ask the oracle yes/no on its own answer). This FAILED: LLM sycophancy confirmed wrong facts 6/6. Self-confirmation does not discriminate. Reverted; logged as a negative with the lesson that the real fix is parsing, not verification.

v4 found the actual root cause: `_parse_answer`'s ASCII word regex was FRAGMENTING accented words -- "espa(n-tilde)ol" split to ['espa','ol'], and the last-word parser returned 'ol'. The fix is a general Unicode NFKD `_ascii_fold` applied before the regex (n-tilde -> n, a-acute -> a), so "espanol"/"bogota"/"munchen" stay whole. Safe (parse-path only, existing cache hashes unchanged). Unit 10/10. The data-quality problem was a parse bug, not a verification problem.

## 14. Pack 298 + 299 -- Cache Compression And The Device Profile

**298:** `cat4_packed_cache.py` `PackedAnchorCache` replaces the Python dict (123 B/entry of real per-object overhead) with sorted int64 keys + auto-width value-ids + an interned value table, bisect lookup. Measured at 100k: 10.0 B/entry in RAM (12.3x), and 5.5 B/entry when delta-encoded and zlib-compressed (22.5x) -- in the 3-10 B target. A 1B-fact cache: 123 GB -> 5.5 GB stored. Exact recall parity. Minimal-perfect-hash to drop keys entirely is the noted path to ~3 B.

**299:** the device profile. The substrate body is constant in fact count (191 MB), the packed cache grows at ~12 B/entry. A one-million-fact organism totals **203 MB** -- comfortably inside a 512 MB phone budget; ten million totals 311 MB. The small-device story 285.7 set out to tell is told instead by derive-not-store (keeps the fact count low) plus 298 compression plus this profile.

## 16. Pack 303 -- Real SimpleQA Benchmark (Honest)

The official OpenAI SimpleQA dataset (4326 questions) was downloaded and the Pack 296 continual-learning pipeline run on a 60-question sample. Phase 0 cold substrate-only: 0/60. Phase 1 one self-teach pass: 47 facts active-learned. Phase 2 post-teach substrate-only: 1/60 = 2%. The honest result -- 0% -> 2% on REAL SimpleQA, against 296's 0% -> 85% on easy capitals -- is diagnostic. Two bottlenecks: the 8B R1-distill teacher's accuracy is low on obscure SimpleQA facts, and recall returns noise-floor tokens rather than the taught value for multi-word gold answers ("Dr. Kalam", "May 16, 2015") behind long questions. The continual-learning mechanism works; real-SimpleQA performance is bottlenecked by teacher capacity and multi-word-answer storage, both real and both fixable. A publishable baseline, reported without inflation.

## 17. Free N-Gram Fluency -- Negative (The Final Chase)

The day's last effort, at Prince's request, was to make free novel-recombination generation cohere. The first attempt absorbed a teacher corpus into the language bank, but the corpus was R1 chain-of-thought ramble, and generation was byte-identical (the junk-saturated cooccur bank dominated). The second attempt fixed both -- self-distilled a clean corpus from the organism's own templated sentences and absorbed into a fresh clean lang bank. This escaped the "so/if/would" attractor (real domain words appeared: "the capital of sweden is europe...") but the n-gram walk still collapsed on function words ("the the of is", unique-ratio 0.55, valid-bigram 44%). The deeper finding: FHRR n-gram recall over a modest bank is blurry -- high-frequency function words dominate `combined_ngram_candidates`, and they cannot be down-weighted without a hardcoded stopword list (against the standing rule). Free fluency is a real multi-pack arc -- it needs sharper decoding (resonator cleanup at the generation step, or the frame-bigram FSM actually locked), far more clean data, and higher-order-gram weighting. Schema-framed and learned-template generation remain the shipped, working fluency path. Negative recorded.
