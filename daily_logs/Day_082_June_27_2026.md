## 1. Objective

Day 81 crossed the bridge to data; Day 82 began with the obvious next question --
can the organism, now fed real knowledge, beat a transformer on a reasoning task
the world recognises? The founder's standing requirement framed the entire day:
a free organism that runs on a phone but answers worse than a paid model is
worthless, so the goal is not a niche win but to match frontier knowledge by
scale and then do the best reasoning in the world on top of it. The day's work
separated those two halves -- knowledge, which needs scale and funding, and
reasoning, which does not -- and set out to prove the reasoning half now.

The path was not straight. A long, honest investigation of CommonsenseQA
established where the wall actually is, several promising mechanisms were tried
and measured and found wanting, and only after that discipline did the right
experiment present itself: isolate reasoning from knowledge entirely by giving
both the organism and a frontier LLM the same invented facts, and measure who
reasons more reliably as the chain deepens. That experiment produced the day's
headline and the project's first head-to-head victory over a frontier model.

## 2. Public Repository Refresh, The Multi-Value Fix, And The Ask Role

The public repository (`HitoshiFTW/Ikigai-Main`) was refreshed from its stale
Day-58 snapshot to the current framework, with a one-command `benchmark.py` that
passes ten of ten headline checks and an honest, benchmark-forward README. In the
course of that refresh a real bug surfaced and was fixed as Pack 330: the
`CompactAnchorCache` was single-value last-wins, so `atoms()` and `knows()` only
ever returned one value per relation despite the Pack-329 claim of a multi-value
meaning web. The cache was given a multi-value payload joined by a unit
separator, with an `add_value` method and the guarantee that `entry[-1]` still
returns the most-recently-written value so every existing reader's last-wins
semantics are preserved. After the fix, `cat hasa tail AND whiskers` surfaces
correctly; regression held.

Pack 331 built the interrogative `ask` role. The organism already mapped every
relation to a question template (the relation-to-question direction); what it
lacked was the inverse -- recognising, from a natural-language question, which
typed relation is being asked for. The new `ikigai/cognition/ask_role.py` learns
that mapping from data, binding a question's cue tokens to the relation it asks
for through the organism's own `MultiRoleMemory`, with no hardcoded cue-to-relation
table. Traced on real questions it correctly maps "where", "find", and "at" to
the location relation. It is exposed as `org.learn_ask` and `org.ask_relation`.

## 3. Constant-Speed Unbounded Learning, Proven

A foundational claim of the architecture -- that the organism learns at constant
speed and bounded RAM regardless of how much it has already absorbed -- was put
to a direct test and confirmed. The naive co-occurrence training path degrades
badly (from 466 to 159 sentences per second as vocabulary grows), but the
degradation is not in the substrate; it is in three throwaway side-caches
(ComputedKey's per-word key cache and the SDM location caches) that bloat and
provoke garbage-collection thrash. Routing training through the existing
FlatTrainer, which compacts those caches at flush boundaries, absorbed one
million Tatoeba sentences in 6.1 minutes at a flat two-to-three thousand
sentences per second with bounded RAM, lifting the organism's co-occurrence
vocabulary from 6,808 to 53,996. The substrate write is O(1) by construction;
only the convenience caches grew, and bounding them restores constant speed. This
is the structural property no transformer has: knowledge can be added live, on a
device, with no retraining.

## 4. The CommonsenseQA Investigation -- A Wall Mapped Completely

The bulk of the day was an honest, exhaustive investigation of CommonsenseQA on
its real validation set of 1,221 questions. The first family of approaches --
treating the question as a bag of words, a sequence, or a distributional context,
with or without crosstalk cleanup, at d=400 or d=1024 -- all scored near the
twenty-percent random floor. The founder rejected the temptation to quit and
insisted the answer lay in copying biology; that pressure produced the first
crack. Spreading activation over the ConceptNet graph with convergence scoring
(the Collins-Loftus mechanism combined with coincidence detection) lifted the
number to 30.7%, the first movement all day. Typed relation-paths with gradient
boosting then broke the ceiling to 35.9%. Lowering the edge-weight threshold to
admit more data hurt rather than helped, establishing that quality beats quantity
for this signal.

The founder's intuition that the answer already existed in the directory was
tested directly: his own Day-54-to-56 inventions -- the self-assembling
convergence field, the holographic context encoder, the VSA attention block, the
symbolic world model -- were pulled out and applied to the task. The energy-field
convergence scored 22 to 24 percent, the holographic order-aware encoding 20
percent, both below the simple explicit graph. The conclusion is a clean,
defensible meta-finding, recorded separately: across roughly nine distinct
mechanisms the score caps between 31 and 36 percent, and the simplest mechanism
wins, because every approach reads the same underlying signal -- concept-overlap
between the question's concepts and the candidate answers. The wall is the
SIGNAL, not the inference machinery. CommonsenseQA's missing points are learned
language understanding -- the transformer's actual moat -- and no symbolic
shortcut over ConceptNet supplies them.

Qualitatively, the organism reasons sensibly even at 33 percent: it answers
bookstore for where one finds printed works, mexico for a Spanish-speaking coffee
country, make-peace for what to do with the world for harmony, and lots-of-attention
for what a dog needs. Several of its "wrong" answers are defensible against a
crowdsourced gold label (farming-areas versus midwest; making-music versus
singing), and its confidence is honestly low, the calibration layer working as
intended.

## 5. The Reasoning Engine, Built And Wired

By mid-day the work had fragmented across ten experiment scripts. At the founder's
direction these were consolidated into one permanent component,
`ikigai/cognition/multihop_reasoner.py`. The `MultiHopReasoner` is a four-stage
pipeline -- comprehend (extract seed concepts and the asked relation),
derive (spreading activation over the typed graph), score (typed relation-path
features fused with convergence, combined by learned weights or a plugged
gradient-boosting model), and answer with a calibrated confidence and an
honest-unknown abstention. It is wired into the organism as `org.kg_reasoner`
and `org.reason_mc`, validated end-to-end through that clean API at 32.8 percent
on CommonsenseQA. The engine is deliberately separable from the knowledge it
reasons over: the same `org.reason_mc` will accept ConceptNet today and
frontier-scale knowledge later. This is the permanent foundation on which the
day's headline result was built.

## 6. Native Dopamine Reinforcement On The Organism

The founder asked for reinforcement learning to be native -- the organism should
learn from reward, not batch supervision. The dopamine loop was implemented over
real CommonsenseQA: the organism predicts the relation a question asks for, checks
whether that relation reaches the gold answer in the knowledge graph, and on a
hit reinforces the successful relation while on a miss it reads the gold and
reinforces the relation that reaches it. Over three epochs the reward rate climbed
from seven percent to seventy-two percent -- the loop demonstrably works, native,
reinforcing the organism's own `ask` role and saved back into the body. But
validation accuracy moved only from 19.8 to 22 percent, which is itself an honest
finding: knowing the correct relation does not pick the correct answer, because
the distractors share that relation. The policy was learned; the policy alone is
not the discriminator.

## 7. The Twenty-Thousand Wall, Measured

The founder's standing obsession -- that the substrate is O(1) but capped near
twenty thousand items per role -- was measured directly on the real VSASDM rather
than argued. Capacity is a sharp cliff, a Kanerva phase transition: perfect recall
to roughly five thousand items, collapsing to near zero by twenty thousand at
d=400. Raising the dimension five-fold barely helps (9.5 percent at twenty
thousand), and Hopfield cleanup recovers nothing past the cliff; capacity is bound
by M, the number of hard locations, not by dimension. The conclusion is that
raising M is brute force -- it buys capacity at the cost of RAM -- and the only
O(1)-honest route to scale is derive-not-store: keep the irreducible kernel in a
modest substrate and compute the rest. This settled an architectural tension
explicitly: the explicit cache is a measurement scaffold, the substrate plus
derive-not-store is the production home, and storing everything in an O(N) cache
would betray the constant-RAM thesis and will not be shipped.

## 8. The Head-To-Head Victory Over Frontier Models

The day's discipline finally surfaced the right experiment. Rather than fight the
transformer on language, where it is strongest, the test isolates reasoning from
knowledge entirely. Both systems are given the same set of made-up facts -- random
six-letter names so neither has seen them in training -- forming a chain of
`reportsto` relations of increasing depth, padded with twenty distractor edges as
noise. The question is to follow the chain a given number of steps and name the
endpoint. NeuroSeed answers by exact derivation, one stored-fact lookup per hop;
the LLM must trace the chain in context.

The result is the project's first frontier-beating claim. NeuroSeed holds 100
percent accuracy at every depth from one hop to twenty. Llama-3.3-70B, the weaker
opponent, scores 93 percent at one hop and collapses to 40 percent at two and 13
percent at three. The frontier model, Nemotron-Ultra-550B (GPQA-Diamond 86.7
percent), is genuinely strong and holds a tie through three hops -- then collapses
to 27 percent at five hops and 0 percent at eight.

| Reasoning depth (hops) | NeuroSeed | Nemotron-Ultra-550B | Llama-3.3-70B |
|---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| 1 | 100% | 100% | 93% |
| 2 | 100% | 100% | 40% |
| 3 | 100% | 100% | 13% |
| 5 | 100% | 27% | 13% |
| 8 | 100% | 0% | 20% |

The mechanism is exactly the compounding of per-hop error: an autoregressive chain
of depth n with per-step error p succeeds with probability (1 - p)^n, invisible at
shallow depth and fatal as the chain lengthens, while exact derivation has p = 0
and stays flat. A 550-billion-parameter frontier model answers zero percent of
eight-hop cases; the organism answers all of them, in milliseconds, on a CPU, in
under a megabyte. The claim is held deliberately narrow and is therefore
bulletproof: given clean structured knowledge, the organism reasons reliably and
deeply where a frontier model degrades to nothing. It is not a claim of general
superiority -- large models decisively beat the organism on language comprehension,
open knowledge, fuzzy reasoning, and messy real-world input, and this benchmark
hands the organism clean facts and a clean question. The real-world gap is the
language front-end, which is the next build. A short paper with two figures was
written (`paper/reasoning_edge.md`) and the result was synced to the public
repository.

## 9. Close

The day produced one permanent component (the reasoning engine), one proven native
learning mechanism (the dopamine loop), one complete negative-result map (the
CommonsenseQA representation wall), one settled architectural question (the 20k
wall and derive-not-store), and one defensible frontier-beating result (the
head-to-head). The organism's body was augmented with Tatoeba co-occurrence and a
reward-trained ask role and saved, with a pre-Tatoeba backup retained. The next
moves are a larger paper-grade run of the head-to-head, broader reasoning tasks
beyond single-relation chains, additional frontier opponents, and the language
front-end that would let the organism acquire clean structure from messy input --
the bridge from this narrow, real victory to general use. The knowledge-scale
phase remains the funded next chapter, now earned by a proof a 17-year-old can put
in front of anyone: a phone-sized organism that out-reasons a frontier model.
