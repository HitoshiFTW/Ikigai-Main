## 2. The depth loop — `org.respond`, one loop, the arbiter decides

The goal: understand and respond as one emergent loop, where a small chat and a large artifact are
the same loop at different depth, and the choice is free energy, not a request-type switch.

A first cut had `respond` call the arbiter and then, in a python `if it abstained, then plan`,
decide to decompose. The founder caught it immediately: that `if` is a hand-authored router bolted
on top of the loop — the exact hardcoding the rules forbid. The shipped version removes it. The
decision now lives inside the one loop: `reason()` understands a goal request (the world-model
states it names) and proposes a plan decomposition at its free energy, competing with derive /
multihop / cache. Argmin free energy picks it only when nothing exact clears, so `reason()` returns
`method='planner'` by itself. `org.respond` holds no routing — it only enacts the arbiter's choice:
a direct answer is depth 1; a chosen plan is executed by recursing the same loop per hop and
stitched by the generator, depth 2.

Pixel discipline: the new goal-understanding runs only under a `_deep_dispatch` flag, so flag-off is
byte-identical. `day90_dispatch_pixel` verified 0 answer diffs and 0 method changes across 36
production queries with the flag on vs off; then it was made default. Gate `day90_depth_loop`.
Honest scope: mechanism, toy symbolic domain, demonstrated to depth 2; deeper nesting needs a
two-level world model that a single shared causal model traps on, deferred rather than faked.

## 3. Address-space generation, and the production cache

The founder's idea: a language has a finite lexicon; store every word as an HV and the relations
between words as HV too, but reading and generation should carry the words' ADDRESSES, not the
words — surface strings only at the edges. This is the no-backprop analog of a transformer's
next-token head: next address by resonance over a learned transition memory, decode only to emit.

Built `AddressGenerator` (`holo_generate.py`) + `org.generate_addressed`: a codebook (word ↔ code ↔
key), per-context successor bundles (relations in HV), a walk that carries codes, and a Kanerva-
style successor cache. Gate `day90_address_generation`: address-space confirmed, grounded, coherent,
novel, and warm per-step cost FLAT across a 607× lexicon.

The genuinely useful part shipped into production. The existing generator
(`HolographicBranchGenerator`, backing `generate_fluent` / `generate_novel` / `valid_next`) rebuilt
the whole vocab matrix on every successor call (O(vocab) per step, no cache). Two output-preserving
optimizations were added: build the codebook once, and cache successor sets per context. Every
Day-87/88/89 generation gate stayed green (byte-identical), then it was default. Verified in
`day90_verify_upgrade`: parity with the old logic on all contexts, correct cache invalidation.

Honesty correction on record: I first said this was "O(1)." The founder challenged it. The truth is
O(1) **warm** (cached/repeated contexts) and O(vocab) **cold** (a genuinely novel context still pays
a linear cleanup). The old path was O(vocab) every step; we made repeated contexts O(1). Near-zero
**amortized**, not zero. The cost lives in DRAM (a lazy codebook matrix, ~1.6 GB projected at 500k
words), not disk; disk is unchanged.

## 4. Facts as address-lists (the founder's knowledge idea)

The founder's second framing: a new fact brings no new words — all its words are already addresses
in the fixed lexicon; a fact is a LIST of addresses, and its consequences are derived. Demonstrated
on the substrate (`day90_address_binding`): grew the lexicon by 200,000 words with the fact store
unchanged (words are free computed addresses); a fact is three indices over the shared book; the
consequence closure is derived by address arithmetic and stored zero (+74% derived over stored). The
honest catch, stated in the gate: an arbitrary atomic association still costs one binding — words
are free and consequences are free, but the atomic link carries information and must be stored once.

## 5. Consolidation — hippocampus to cortex, one substrate

A memory-architecture question exposed a real fact: the main store is a Kanerva SDM (VSASDM, 16,384
hard locations + counter bank), the generation store (branch_gen) is a separate transient store, and
they already share the address space (`ck`). Biology does exactly this: specialised memories
(hippocampal sequence, cortical/cerebellar semantic) that share concept addresses and consolidate
during sleep. What was missing was consolidation.

Added `org.consolidate_generation` (wired into `sleep_consolidate`): each generation transition is
replayed into the durable SDM, addressed by the same `ck` keys, so sequence knowledge becomes
persistent (it now lives in .ikg) and lives in one substrate. Gate `day90_consolidation`: 91% recall
/ 100% precision (lossy and distributed by nature — that is what cortical consolidation is), and it
survived save to a temp .ikg and reload into a fresh organism that never saw the corpus (91% recall
from the reloaded SDM alone). A private LSH sub-linear cleanup was built first, then removed as
un-biological duplication — the SDM already is the sparse content-addressable store; biology would
not grow a second one.

## 6. The fluency problem — one rejection, one real lever

The day's real target was fluent generation, and it stayed honest.

**Lever A (context conditioning) — tried and rejected.** The hypothesis: condition the next word on
the whole accumulated context (an attention analog). The naive version — score candidates by
resonance to an accumulated context bundle — mode-collapsed into repetition, and worse, a random
topic assignment is not in the key geometry, so key-resonance cannot track topic. Rejected and
reverted; nothing shipped. The negative result taught the real lesson: local prediction is not the
bottleneck (grounding already gives locally-plausible transitions). The gap is structure and meaning.

**Structure-first generation (Levelt) — shipped.** Biology produces language top-down: build a frame
(a scaffold of typed slots) first, then fill slots by meaning. Added `org.generate_structured`: the
frame is a hard correct-by-construction constraint (an off-type word is never emitted), each slot
filled by a grounded, type-correct word chosen by theme resonance, with inhibition-of-return so
fillers vary. Gate `day90_structure_first`: 100% structural validity vs 38% for the flat theme-walk
under type-ambiguous transitions, grounded, coherent. This unifies the depth loop with the generator
— the frame is a plan (planner-producible): goal → frame → fill → artifact.

**Structure induced from data — shipped.** The remaining honesty gap was that the frame and types
were hand-given. `day90_frame_induction` induces word-types distributionally (a word's signature is
the bundle of its successors' keys; cluster by resonance) at 100% purity with no labels, induces the
frame as the modal type-sequence, and drives `generate_structured` from the induced structure at
100% structural validity. Structure is now learned, then filled by meaning. Mechanism on made-up
data; fluent English still needs a real corpus and a richer type inventory.

## 7. One generation entry — `org.generate`

The founder asked to unify the many generation endpoints, transformer-style. Done without a
god-function: `org.generate` is one public entry that delegates by parameter to the proven
implementations (text / frame-relax, structure-first, address-space, constrained, novel) — the
former methods stay as internals and back-compat. The pre-existing `generate` (frame-relax) was
renamed `generate_frame` and folded in as the `text=` mode (no internal callers; safe). Gate
`day90_unified_generate`: the one entry reproduces every mode byte-for-byte. `org.respond` remains
the top understand-then-act entry that reaches `org.generate` for a generation request.

## 8. Substrate bake-off — one hand vs many hands per clock

The founder's substrate idea: our FHRR is d clocks with one hand each (a unit phasor, one phase per
dimension); would multiple hands (a quaternion — 3 angles per dimension, non-commutative binding)
distinguish better? Tested in isolation, pure math, at equal float storage
(`day90_quaternion_vs_complex`). Result, no glaze: capacity is a wash at equal storage; on order —
the one axis where quaternion could win, since its binding is non-commutative — our existing complex
plus positional permutation separates order **better** (0.99 vs 0.72), and quaternion costs about 4×
the bind compute. Verdict: keep complex FHRR + permutation; the multiple-hands idea is real and
elegant (quaternion/hypercomplex VSA) but does not beat what we have and is not worth a substrate
rewrite. A clean negative result.
