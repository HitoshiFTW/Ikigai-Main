## 1. Objective

Day 83 built the natural-language front door and consolidated the organism into a
single everything-on system, and named the next rung as knowledge at scale on real
data. Day 84 took that literally: the full Wikidata truthy dump, forty-three
gigabytes compressed, was on disk, and the task was to drive it through the
derive-not-store engine on one ordinary CPU core and report -- without softening
the numbers -- how far the storage stays flat while the answerable space grows,
whether the organism genuinely knows what it ingested, and where the pipe still
breaks. The standing requirement governed the day as it has governed the project:
the win is to match frontier knowledge by scale and do the world's best reasoning
on top of it, and a claim only counts if it survives being stated honestly.

---

## 2. The Junk War -- Filtering Raw Truthy

Raw Wikidata truthy is not clean triples; it is a firehose in which the structural
backbone is a thin minority. A single Wikidata item carries on the order of nine
hundred predicates, the overwhelming majority of which -- labels and descriptions
and aliases in every language, sitelinks, and hundreds of external-identifier
strings -- have literal objects that the derive engine cannot chain and the rule
miner cannot use. The first measurement made this concrete: the head of the dump
yielded only fifty-five distinct entities across the first fifty thousand lines,
buried under one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine mostly-junk relations.

Three filters were added to `parse_ntriples` in `kg_ingest.py`, cheapest first. An
object-is-entity filter keeps a triple only when its object resolves to a Wikidata
Q-entity, dropping every literal in one regex test and collapsing the relation set
from thousands to tens. An optional predicate allowlist restricts the stream to a
named backbone -- subclass-of and instance-of for the purest taxonomy. A parse-time
heartbeat reports lines read, edges kept, and rate to standard error so a long scan
is never a black box. A case bug was caught and fixed in the same pass: the
predicate's last URI segment came back capitalised, so the lowercase allowlist
matched nothing and a backbone run silently kept zero edges for four million lines
before the cause was found.

---

## 3. The Quadratic Hang, And The Index That Killed It

With the filters in place the ingest itself was never the bottleneck -- fifty
thousand edges absorbed in just over a second. The freeze was in rule discovery.
On the diluted real graph the pair-miners -- synonymy, inverse, inheritance --
each looped every relation pair against every entity in the store, an
order-relations-squared-times-entities scan that, even pruned to a hundred surviving
relations against a hundred-thousand-odd entities, became hundreds of millions of
atom lookups and ran for fifteen minutes without printing a line.

Two fixes brought it to heel, both native and both general beyond this run. First,
a low-support prune in `discover`: a relation that appears on fewer subjects than
the support threshold can never reach that threshold in any rule, so it is dropped
before the quadratic begins, which on raw truthy removes the long tail of one-off
identifier predicates outright. Second, a subject index in `RuleMiner`: each miner
now iterates only the entities that actually carry the relation -- and, for a pair,
their intersection -- instead of every entity in the store, turning a scan over all
entities into a scan over the handful that matter. A cost-guard sits above the
pair-miners: when the estimated pair cost exceeds a budget, the run falls back to
the transitive miner alone, which is linear in the facts and is the only rule the
closure and multiplier count actually consume. Below the budget -- the clean, small
knowledge bases of the regression gates -- the full mine still runs exactly as
before.

## 4. The Scale Result, Stated Honestly

The pipe then ran end to end. Two million entity-to-entity edges, filtered from
twenty-five million lines of the truthy dump, were ingested in a hundred and
forty-five seconds on one core -- about sixteen thousand edges per second. The
stored kernel was one million five hundred and thirty-eight thousand atoms across
three hundred and four thousand entities and one thousand three hundred and eleven
relations, with fifty-six transitive rules discovered, occupying ten and a half
megabytes. Counting the complete legitimate answerable space -- forward transitive
closure, reverse closure, pairwise same-attribute comparisons, and cross-relation
two-hop chains, all derived and none stored -- gave two billion nine million
answerable facts, a multiplier of one thousand three hundred and six fold,
exceeding the three-hundred-and-fifty-three-fold figure measured on ConceptNet. The
projection that follows is that a billion facts would need roughly seven hundred and
sixty-five thousand atoms, about five megabytes.

The number was reported with its breakdown rather than as a headline, because the
breakdown is the honest part. Of the two billion answerable facts, two billion and
five million -- ninety-nine point seven percent -- are pairwise comparisons, the
cheap class: any two entities that share a relation and value can be compared, and
the count grows as the square of each group regardless of any deep derivation. The
genuine derive-not-store work, the forward and reverse transitive closure that the
sub-megabyte claim rests on, is about two and a third million facts, a multiplier of
roughly one-and-a-half-fold on its own. The conclusion is the same as it was on
ConceptNet and is recorded without spin: the "billion facts under a megabyte" target
is not met on truthy, at five megabytes, because truthy's subclass tree is shallow
and sparse and the comparison count carries the headline. Reaching sub-megabyte
needs dense subclass depth with attributes that inherit down it -- the backbone path,
or a curated taxonomy -- not comparison inflation.

## 5. The Organism Knows -- Recall And Derivation On Real Entities

A multiplier is an accounting claim; the organism still had to be shown actually
answering. A single pass over the head of the dump collected, co-located per
subject, both the English labels and the backbone edges, so the same entities that
were ingested could be named back. Exact recall held: asked what vanadium is, the
organism returns metal; Mount Vesuvius, mountain; the GIF, a video compression
format; 10 Hygiea, an asteroid; Sierra Leone and Sudan, republics. These are real
facts, retrieved exactly from the cache and translated back to English from the
dump's own labels.

Multi-hop subclass chains were then derived from the transitive closure the
organism never stored: platinum to precious metal to metal; hypomania to mood
disorder to mental disorder; an isotope of helium to isotope to nuclide. The first
attempt printed the deepest classes as raw Q-codes because their labels lay past the
twelve-million-line slice that had been scanned, and the codes themselves came back
split by a space -- the long-known digit-tokenizer behaviour, splitting a Q-code's
letter from its digits on the value path. A second ingest that stored the entities
as their English labels from the outset removed the problem at the root: the
organism stored "vanadium isa metal," every chain came out fully named, and there
were no Q-codes left to mangle.

---

## 6. The Gap At The Front Door

One honest gap surfaced and is recorded as the next rung. The deterministic recall
path -- a direct atom lookup against a known relation -- is exact. The
natural-language front door is not yet wired to reach it: asked "what is vanadium"
in plain prose, the question parser returns nothing, because the stored relation,
isa, is not a word in the question, and the emergent parser keys relations off the
words present. Bridging a bare wh-question onto the most-general stored relation,
natively and without an authored template table, is the first design item below.
The capability behind the door works; the door's latch does not yet catch.

## 7. Strategy Made Precise

Much of the day was the founder pressing the architecture against hard questions,
and the answers are worth recording because they set the direction.

On capacity, the framing was corrected: the hundred-and-eighty-two-megabyte
organism file does not hold bulk knowledge by cramming it into a fixed substrate.
There are two stores. The superposition banks are fixed and bounded -- roughly
twenty thousand items per role before crosstalk -- and serve binding and cognition,
not bulk. The anchor cache and atom store grow linearly beside them at about seven
bytes per fact, disk-bound, not file-bound. Knowledge therefore scales in the cheap
cache next to the fixed body, a gigabyte holding on the order of a hundred and forty
million atoms and, at the day's multiplier, on the order of a hundred and eighty
billion answerable facts.

On the compute a frontier corpus would cost, the asymmetry was made concrete. To
train a multi-trillion-parameter model on a hundred-terabyte corpus is on the order
of ten-to-the-thirty-one floating-point operations -- months of a datacenter of
GPUs, millions of dollars, many epochs of backpropagation over every parameter. The
organism reads the same corpus once, on CPU, with no backpropagation: the dominant
cost is extraction of triples from messy text, embarrassingly parallel across cheap
cores; ingest of clean triples runs at the sixteen thousand per second measured this
day. And because identical things bind to the same deterministic key, a fact
mentioned a hundred thousand times across the corpus is stored once -- the organism's
effective work scales with the number of distinct facts, with novelty, not with
volume, the way a brain does not relearn that fire is hot each time it hears it.

On where the organism could live, the same point inverts: a query's cost is constant
in the size of the corpus that built the kernel -- one content-addressed lookup plus
a short derivation chain, milliseconds on a microcontroller -- so the binding
constraint on a device is its memory, not its compute. A drone carrying a
hundred-megabyte domain kernel answers billions of derived facts offline,
restartable from a single memory-mapped file, while a datacenter node holds the whole
thing; both answer at the same per-query cost.

On the memory itself, the founder named a north-star worth its own note: a flat
memory in which a query lifts only the queried fact and its chain, leaving everything
else at rest. The anchor cache already does this -- a hash to an exact slot, no scan.
The dense superposition banks do not, because reading one superposed fact correlates
against the whole bank. The resolution is the sparse, distributed, content-addressed
memory the holographic reader is already partly built on -- Kanerva's own design, in
which a query activates only the locations near its address, so only the addressed
assembly rises, the way one memory in a brain fires one assembly and leaves the
other eighty-six billion neurons at baseline. This was saved as a standing memory
and is the third flatness: storage flat by derive-not-store, the answerable space
huge, and access flat by sparse activation.

On the contest with transformers, the founder asked whether their home turf -- raw
fluency -- could be taken without breaking the project's rules, and the honest answer
was that it could not, precisely because raw fluency at open-domain scale is itself
the scaling game the rules were written to refuse. Wanting both a cheap, fixed,
untrained substrate and victory at the one thing that billions of trained parameters
produce is a contradiction, and the right response is not to chase it but to redefine
the battle: generation is not only fluency but faithfulness, grounding, instruction
consistency and the absence of hallucination, and on those benchmarks a substrate
that retrieves and derives rather than generates can win within the rules. The path
to fluent-enough output is a thin non-backpropagation read-out -- the reservoir-and-
recursive-least-squares verbaliser already built earlier in the project -- over an
exact substrate, fluent and never hallucinating, a guarantee no trained model can
make. We do not out-bullshit the champion bullshitter; we are right and articulate
enough, which is what a brain is.

On the inventions, the ledger was read back. Of the canonical five -- the residue
arithmetic ring, the substrate-native write gate, the cleanup-wall bypass, the
organism reinforcement loop, and the closure-proof operator -- four are shipped and
running and only the fifth, a six-month theoretical proof, is unbuilt, and it is not
on the critical path. The larger compression moonshot of stacked invention pathways,
and the entire effort to compress a language model into the substrate, were marked
not-needed: the narrow reasoning win and the derive-not-store multiplier already
stand without them. The single invention genuinely worth building next is the
flat-access sparse memory named above, because it serves the proven thesis rather
than an abandoned ambition.

## 8. Hygiene And The Regression Gate

The production organism was never touched. Every run today built a throwaway
instance in memory, ingested Wikidata into it, measured, and exited; no save was
called, and `organism.ikg` remains the clean Day-82 file. A Wikidata-loaded
persistent organism, if wanted, is a deliberate future step writing to a separate
file, not an overwrite of the clean production knowledge.

The day's permanent code edits touched the production path -- the N-triples parser
and its filters, the low-support prune in `discover`, the subject index and
cost-guard in `RuleMiner`, and the progress hooks in `ingest_triples` -- so the
regression gate was run to confirm none of it disturbed the live organism. It held:
the production organism loads in two and a half seconds at a hundred and ninety-two
megabytes, answers twenty of twenty-one capitals at under a millisecond each, closes
the arithmetic ring eight of eight, abstains honestly on an unknown entity, and runs
all ten wired capabilities live. Two demonstration scripts and the scale-run flags
were added under `experiments/`, where they are unrestricted.

## 9. What Day 85 Inherits

The pipe is proven on real data at scale and the organism is shown to know what it
ingested and to derive what it never stored, with the storage staying flat. Four
design items are queued in priority order. First, the natural-language bridge that
maps a bare question onto the stored relation, natively, so the front door latches.
Second, the dense-inheritance path to sub-megabyte -- mining attributes that inherit
down the subclass tree and self-compressing, which is the real lever the comparison
count has been masking. Third, the flat-access sparse memory, the day's named
north-star and the next invention worth building. Fourth, the grounded read-out that
turns exact answers into fluent, faithful language. The claim stays narrow and the
numbers stay honest; that discipline is the asset.
